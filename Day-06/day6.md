# Day 6 — Resume Optimization with Claude

## Objective

Today I used Claude to analyze my resume for ATS compatibility and generate an optimized one-page version.

The goal was to understand how AI can improve resume structure, keyword visibility, and ATS readability without fabricating information.

## Original Resume

I uploaded my existing resume to Claude and asked it to perform an ATS-focused resume optimization.

### Original ATS Score

**48/100**

The original resume used a two-column/sidebar-style layout with icons and visual elements that could be difficult for ATS parsers to read reliably.

## ATS Analysis by Claude

Claude identified several areas for improvement:

- Removed the two-column sidebar layout and changed it to a single-column linear layout.
- Removed icons and graphics placed next to section labels.
- Added a **Professional Summary** containing relevant skills and keywords.
- Converted project descriptions into consistent action-verb bullet points such as **Developed, Implemented, Designed, Built, and Integrated**.
- Standardized contact information into a plain-text line below the name.
- Combined Tech Skills, Soft Skills, and Languages into one clearly labeled **Skills** section.
- Replaced stylized section labels with standard ATS-friendly headings such as **Professional Summary, Education, Projects, and Skills**.
- Did not add Certifications or Experience because they were not present in the original resume.

## Optimized Resume

Claude generated a new **single-page A4 DOCX resume** using a simple single-column format without tables, icons, or text boxes.

### Optimized ATS Score

**88/100**

This represents an improvement of **40 points** from the original score.

### Score Improvement

**48/100 → 88/100**

The main improvement came from making the resume easier for ATS systems to parse while improving keyword matching and structure.

## Additional Suggestions from Claude

Claude also suggested areas that could improve the resume further when real information becomes available:

- Add a **Certifications** section for relevant completed certifications.
- Add an **Experience** section after gaining internship, freelance, or hackathon experience.
- Add live links/GitHub links to projects such as GyanSetu or MindMash if they are deployed or publicly available.

Claude specifically noted that these should only be added when the information is genuine.

## Key Learnings

### 1. ATS readability matters

A visually attractive resume is not necessarily ATS-friendly. Simple layouts, standard headings, and readable text structures can improve parsing.

### 2. Keywords should be easy to identify

Adding a focused Professional Summary and organizing skills under a standard Skills section can make relevant keywords easier for ATS systems to detect.

### 3. Action verbs strengthen project descriptions

Using clear action verbs such as **Developed, Implemented, Designed, Built, and Integrated** makes project descriptions more consistent and impact-oriented.

### 4. AI should optimize, not fabricate

Claude did not invent certifications or work experience that were missing from the original resume. It instead flagged them as areas to improve later with genuine information.

## Biggest Takeaway

The biggest lesson from Day 6 was that **resume optimization is not only about making a resume look better**.

It is about making the information easy for both **ATS systems and recruiters to understand, scan, and match with a job description**.

Claude helped identify structural and keyword-related improvements while keeping the content grounded in the information already present in my resume.
