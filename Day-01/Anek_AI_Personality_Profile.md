## 2. Strengths & Weaknesses

**Strengths**
- Bias toward action — you'd rather ship a rough version than perfect a plan
- Genuine curiosity about *how AI is actually used*, not just how it works in theory
- Comfortable stitching together multiple domains (dev + AI + automation) instead of staying in one lane

**Weaknesses (real ones, not the fake "I work too hard" kind)**

- No track record yet (that you've shown me) of finishing and shipping a complete project end-to-end — the profile right now is built on *intent*, not evidence
- Risk of tool-hopping: agentic AI, automation, web dev, and "AI products" are each deep enough to specialize in — right now you're sampling all four

**The Apprentice Systems-Builder.** Not a prompt-tinkerer, not a pure researcher, not yet an engineer with a portfolio. You're in the phase where AI is a **force multiplier for learning-by-doing** — you use it to build faster than your current skill level would otherwise allow, which is smart, as long as you're also absorbing *why* things work, not just copying what works.

- **AI Product Engineer** — building actual applications on top of LLMs/agents (fits your "practical products" interest directly)
- **AI Automation / Workflow Engineer** — designing agentic pipelines for businesses (huge, underserved market right now)

## 5. Learning & Decision-Making Style

**Learning:** Project-driven, non-linear, "just-in-time" theory. You learn a concept because a build demanded it, not because a syllabus scheduled it.

**Decision-making:** Curiosity-led with a practicality filter — you're drawn to what's new and interesting, but you filter through "can I actually build something real with this?" That's a healthy filter. It stops curiosity from becoming pure distraction.

## 6. What Makes You Different From Most AI Users

Most people using AI tools fall into two camps: (1) passive consumers who ask AI to do their thinking for them, or (2) prompt-hobbyists who never connect it to a real skill stack. You're neither — you're explicitly trying to use AI as scaffolding *while* building your own underlying competence, and you asked for honest calibration instead of flattery. That last part specifically is the differentiator. Most people never ask "where am I actually weak" — they ask "tell me I'm good."

## 7. What You Need to Improve to Become Elite

1. **Finish and ship one full project end-to-end** — not a tutorial clone, something with your own decisions in it, deployed and shareable
2. **Pick a narrower lane for 3–6 months** — agentic AI *or* automation *or* web dev as the primary, others as supporting skills, instead of four parallel fronts
3. **Build a habit of reading source docs/papers, not just AI explanations of them** — AI-explained knowledge is a starting point, not a ceiling
4. **Learn to debug without AI first** — the elite users can solve without the tool; the tool then makes them faster, not dependent
